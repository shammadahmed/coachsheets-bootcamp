## Why Values Matter in Coaching

Values are the deeply held principles that guide our decisions, shape our identity, and determine what we find meaningful. They operate below the surface — most clients have never explicitly articulated their values, yet their values drive every major life choice they make.

When a client's actions are aligned with their values, they experience a sense of purpose, energy, and authenticity. When their actions conflict with their values — even if those actions appear "successful" by external metrics — they feel stuck, restless, empty, or confused.

This is the core insight of values-based coaching: **the feeling of being "stuck" is almost always a values misalignment in disguise.**

**Common presentations of values misalignment:**
- "I have a great job, good salary, nice colleagues... but I feel empty."
- "I know I should be grateful, but I feel restless and dissatisfied."
- "I achieved everything I set out to achieve, and now I do not know what to do."
- "I keep sabotaging myself when things go well."

In each case, the client's current life context is out of sync with one or more core values. The coach's job is to help them discover which values are being compromised and coach them toward realignment.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Values are not goals. Values are ongoing qualities of being (e.g., integrity, connection, growth, contribution). Goals are specific achievements (e.g., "get promoted by June"). Goals can be completed; values are continuous directions. Confusing the two is a common source of post-achievement emptiness.

## Values Clarification Process

Values clarification is not about telling clients what their values should be. It is a discovery process.

### Step 1: Broad Exploration

Ask questions that surface the client's implicit values:

- "When have you felt most fulfilled? What was present in that moment?"
- "What do you admire most in others? What does that say about what you value?"
- "What makes you angry or frustrated? Anger is often a values violation."
- "If you could change one thing about the world, what would it be?"
- "What did you love doing as a child, before you cared what others thought?"

### Step 2: Structured Sorting

Use a Values Card Sort (available in CoachSheets) or a written list of common values. There are hundreds of values; the goal is to narrow to 5–10 core values.

**Suggested process:**
1. Review a list of 50+ values (e.g., authenticity, belonging, creativity, discipline, family, freedom, growth, health, honesty, independence, integrity, leadership, learning, love, stability, security, service, spontaneity).
2. Sort into three piles: "Very important to me," "Somewhat important," "Not important."
3. From the "Very important" pile, select the top 10.
4. From the top 10, select the top 5.
5. For each of the top 5, write one sentence: "For me, [value] means [definition]."
6. Rank them in order of priority.

**The key question:** "If you had to choose between Value A and Value B, which would you choose?" This forced-choice process reveals the true hierarchy. Stated values and enacted values are often different.

### Step 3: Gap Analysis

Once the top values are identified, assess alignment:

- "On a scale of 0–10, how much is this value being lived in your life right now?"
- "Where is the biggest gap between what you value and how you are living?"
- "What would a 10 on this value look like in practice?"
- "What is one thing you could change this week to close the gap?"

The gap analysis often reveals the precise source of the client's stuck feeling. A client who values "creativity" but works in a compliance-heavy role will feel restless. A client who values "family" but works 70-hour weeks will feel conflicted.

## Values-Aligned Goal Setting

Once values are clarified, goals become much easier to set — and much more likely to generate genuine fulfillment.

**Traditional goal setting:** "I want to earn $200,000 per year."
**Values-aligned goal setting:** "I value freedom and security. Earning $200,000 per year would give me the financial freedom to travel and the security to feel stable. The goal serves the values."

### The Values-Goals Alignment Check

For any goal the client sets, run it through this check:

1. **Which value does this goal serve?** (If you cannot name a value, the goal may not be meaningful.)
2. **Does this goal compromise any other value?** (A promotion that requires 70-hour weeks may conflict with the value of "family.")
3. **Is the goal worth the cost?** (Every choice has an opportunity cost. Does the value gained outweigh the value sacrificed?)
4. **How will you maintain your values while pursuing this goal?** (What boundaries, practices, or non-negotiables will protect your values?)

> [!WARNING]
> A client may discover that their current life is built around someone else's values — a parent's, a partner's, or society's. This can be a painful realization. The coach's role is to hold compassionate space, not to push the client into immediate upheaval. Values discovery can lead to significant life changes; the timing and pace belong to the client.

## The VIA Character Strengths Framework

The VIA (Values in Action) framework, developed by psychologists Christopher Peterson and Martin Seligman, identifies 24 universal character strengths organized under six virtues. While values are about what we *prize and prioritize*, character strengths are about what is *strongest in us as individuals*.

The VIA framework complements values-based coaching by helping clients identify their natural strengths and use them more intentionally. Clients who use their top character strengths report higher well-being, engagement, and meaning.

**The 24 VIA Character Strengths (by virtue):**
- **Wisdom:** Creativity, Curiosity, Judgment, Love of Learning, Perspective
- **Courage:** Bravery, Perseverance, Honesty, Zest
- **Humanity:** Love, Kindness, Social Intelligence
- **Justice:** Teamwork, Fairness, Leadership
- **Temperance:** Forgiveness, Humility, Prudence, Self-Regulation
- **Transcendence:** Appreciation of Beauty, Gratitude, Hope, Humor, Spirituality

**How to use VIA in coaching:**
1. Ask the client to complete the free VIA survey at [VIA Character Strengths](https://www.viacharacter.org/).
2. Review the top 5 signature strengths together.
3. Ask: "How are you currently using these strengths? Where could you use them more?"
4. Design goals that leverage the client's strengths rather than compensating for weaknesses.

## Common Values Conflicts in Coaching

| **Conflict** | **Example** | **Coaching Approach** |
|---|---|---|
| Security vs. Freedom | "I want to start a business but I am afraid of losing financial stability." | Explore the hierarchy: which is more important right now? Can you have both in stages? |
| Achievement vs. Connection | "I want to excel in my career, but my family feels neglected." | Redefine success to include connection. Set boundaries that protect both values. |
| Integrity vs. Belonging | "My team does things I disagree with, but I want to fit in." | Explore the cost of compromising integrity. What kind of belonging is worth that cost? |
| Growth vs. Contentment | "I always feel like I should be improving. I never feel satisfied." | Is growth a genuine value or an avoidance of stillness? Can both coexist? |
