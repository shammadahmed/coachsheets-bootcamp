# Module 4, Lesson 2: During-Session Structure

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Structure a coaching session using the four-phase arc: opening, exploration, action, closing
- Allocate time effectively within a 45-minute session without feeling rushed
- Recognize when to follow an emergent client need over the planned agenda
- Close a session with clear commitments and an acknowledgment that lands

---

## The Session Arc

Every coaching session, regardless of the client, topic, or coaching model, benefits from a clear structure. Structure creates safety. When a client knows what to expect in a session, they can relax into the work rather than wondering where the conversation is going.

The session arc we teach has four phases. Think of them as movements in a piece of music—each has a distinct purpose, and they flow naturally from one to the next.

### Phase 1: Opening (5-7 minutes)

The opening sets the tone for everything that follows. It has two parts.

**Check-in:** Begin with a genuine check-in that goes deeper than "how are you?" Ask something specific that connects to the client's recent work:

- "What has been alive for you since our last session?"
- "As you look back on the week, what stands out most?"
- "I noticed your worksheet mentioned a breakthrough with your team. Can you tell me more about that day?"

The check-in is not small talk. It is the first opportunity to gather data about where the client is today. Pay attention to energy level, tone, and what the client chooses to lead with.

**Set the agenda:** After the check-in, collaborate with the client on the session agenda. This is not you announcing what you will cover—it is a shared decision:

- "We have about 40 minutes today. Based on your worksheet and what you've shared so far, what feels most important to work on?"
- "I noticed two themes in your submission: the conflict with your colleague and the question about your next career move. Which one would serve you most to explore today?"

Collaborative agenda-setting ensures the client is invested in the session direction and surfaces what is most alive for them right now.

### Phase 2: Exploration (20-25 minutes)

This is the heart of the session. The exploration phase is where deep questioning, framework application, and genuine discovery happen. Your role here is to:

**Listen for the gap.** What is the distance between where the client is and where they want to be? Listen for discrepancies between stated goals and actual behavior, between values and actions, between what the client says and what their body language conveys.

**Ask follow-up questions.** The best exploration questions build on what the client has just said:

- "You mentioned feeling 'stuck' — what specifically is stuck? And what part of you is trying to move?"
- "Say more about that moment when you decided not to speak up. What was going through your mind?"
- "That's a pattern I've noticed across our last three sessions. What do you make of that?"

**Apply frameworks judiciously.** If you have a coaching framework (GROW, cognitive reframing, values clarification, etc.) that fits the moment, introduce it. But the framework serves the client, not the other way around. If a framework feels forced, drop it.

**Hold space for silence.** Silence is not empty—it is where the client is processing. After asking a powerful question, resist the urge to fill the silence. Count to ten if you need to. Many coaches speak too soon and rob the client of the chance to go deeper.

> "The greatest gift you can give another is the purity of your attention." — Richard Moss

### Phase 3: Action (8-10 minutes)

Exploration without action is navel-gazing. The action phase moves the session from insight to impact.

**Synthesize what emerged:** Briefly summarize what came up in exploration. "So what I'm hearing is that the core issue isn't really about time management—it's about saying no to requests that don't align with your values. Does that land?"

**Co-create next steps:** Ask the client what they want to commit to before the next session. These should be:

- **Specific:** "I will have the conversation" is too vague. "I will schedule a 30-minute conversation with my manager by Thursday at 3pm" is specific.
- **Achievable:** The commitment should stretch the client but not break them.
- **Measurable:** Both you and the client need to know whether it was done.
- **Aligned:** The commitment should connect to the client's broader goals and values.

Not every session needs a formal action item. Sometimes the action is reflection: "Between now and our next session, I want you to notice how many times you minimize your own contributions in meetings. Do not change anything—just notice."

**Assign between-session work if appropriate:** This might be a worksheet, a journaling prompt, or a behavioral experiment. CoachSheets allows you to assign worksheets directly from the session interface.

### Phase 4: Closing (3-5 minutes)

The closing phase is brief but essential. It has three parts:

**Summary:** Briefly restate what the client worked on and what they committed to. This reinforces the learning and ensures alignment. "So today we explored the pattern of taking on other people's urgency, and you are committing to blocking two hours of focused work each morning starting Monday."

**Acknowledgment:** Acknowledge the client's courage, honesty, or effort. This is different from praise (which evaluates) — acknowledgment sees and names what the client has done. "I want to acknowledge how honest you were today about the fear underneath your perfectionism. That takes real courage."

**Logistics:** Confirm the next session date and any between-session work.

## Time Allocation for a 45-Minute Session

Here is a practical breakdown:

| Phase | Duration | Watch for |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Opening (check-in + agenda) | 5-7 min | Don't let check-in expand into exploration |
| Exploration | 20-25 min | This is your main container — protect it |
| Action (synthesis + commitments) | 8-10 min | Leave enough time; do not shortchange this |
| Closing (summary + acknowledgment) | 3-5 min | Never skip the acknowledgment |

These are guidelines, not rigid rules. Some sessions will naturally need more exploration and less action. Some will need a longer opening. The key is awareness: know where you are in the arc at all times.

## Managing Time Without Feeling Rushed

The biggest challenge new coaches face in session is time management. Here are practical strategies:

**Use a visible timer.** Put a clock or timer where you can see it without the client noticing. Check it periodically, not obsessively.

**Develop transition phrases.** When you need to move from exploration to action, use phrases that honor what the client has shared while pointing forward: "This is really rich, and I want to make sure we have time to land it. What is the most important insight you're taking from this conversation?"

**Protect the closing.** If you are running short on time, do not skip the closing. Instead, offer a compressed closing: "We have two minutes left. In one sentence, what is your key takeaway from today? And what is one thing you'll do before we meet again?"

**When to hold the agenda and when to drop it:** The art of time management in coaching is knowing when to follow an emergent thread. Ask yourself: *Is what is emerging right now more important than what I had planned?* If the answer is yes, drop the agenda and follow the energy. You can always pick up the planned topic in the next session.

---

## When to Abandon the Agenda Entirely

There are times when a client arrives in crisis, deep distress, or a major life event. In those moments, the session arc changes. The exploration phase becomes the entire session. Action may be limited to immediate support and resources. Do not try to force the four-phase structure on a client who needs something different. Your job is to be present.

Signs it is time to abandon the agenda:

- The client is in acute emotional distress
- A major life event has just occurred (loss, diagnosis, job loss)
- The client reveals something they have never shared before and needs space to process
- The energy in the room shifts dramatically and the planned agenda feels irrelevant

## Summary

A clear session structure—opening, exploration, action, closing—provides the container for transformative coaching work. Within that structure, the coach must be flexible, willing to follow what emerges, and disciplined about time. The goal is not to execute a perfect plan but to serve the client's growth within a reliable, professional container.
