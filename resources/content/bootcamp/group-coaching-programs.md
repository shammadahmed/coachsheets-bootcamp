# Lesson 5: Group Coaching Programs

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- Compare group coaching with 1:1 coaching and identify when each is appropriate
- Structure a group coaching program with a clear curriculum and cadence
- Use CoachSheets Groups and Assignments features for cohort-based delivery
- Differentiate between mastermind, group coaching, and workshop formats

## Group Coaching vs 1:1 Coaching

Most coaches start with 1:1 coaching because it is the simplest model. One client, one session, one relationship. But as your practice grows, group coaching becomes an essential tool for scaling your impact and income.

### Advantages of Group Coaching

- **Leverage**: You serve 6-12 clients in the same time you would serve one. A $500/month group of 8 generates $4,000/month versus $2,000/month from two 1:1 clients.
- **Peer learning**: Clients learn from each other's insights and experiences. Often, a comment from another group member lands more powerfully than anything the coach says.
- **Community**: Clients feel part of something larger. The accountability of a group keeps members engaged between sessions.
- **Lower price point**: Group coaching is more accessible, allowing you to serve clients who cannot afford premium 1:1 rates.
- **Scalable delivery**: Once you design a group program curriculum, you can run it repeatedly with new cohorts.

### Advantages of 1:1 Coaching

- **Deep customization**: Every session is tailored to the individual client's exact situation.
- **Stronger relationship**: The intimacy of a private coaching relationship builds deep trust.
- **Higher price point**: Clients pay a premium for undivided attention.
- **More flexibility**: You can pivot the session based on what comes up in the moment.

### When to Use Each

Most successful coaches use both models. A typical hybrid practice might include:

- 5-10 1:1 clients at premium rates (core revenue)
- 1-2 group programs running per quarter (scalable growth)
- Workshops or courses (passive or semi-passive)

The 1:1 work funds your practice; the group work amplifies your reach.

## How to Structure a Group Program

A group program is not just a series of group calls. It is a structured container with a clear beginning, middle, and end.

### Duration and Cadence

| Program Type | Duration | Session Cadence |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| Short intensive | 4 weeks | Weekly 60-90 min calls |
| Standard cohort | 8-12 weeks | Weekly 60-90 min calls |
| Long-term | 6 months | Bi-weekly 90 min calls |

### Curriculum Design

Each session in your group program should have:

1. **A theme**: A clear topic for the session (e.g., "Identifying Your Core Values").
2. **A pre-session worksheet**: Sent via CoachSheets before the session so members arrive prepared.
3. **Teaching content**: 15-20 minutes of framework or concept delivery.
4. **Group exercise**: A breakout room discussion, a worksheet done together, or a peer coaching round.
5. **Individual coaching**: 2-3 live coaching spots per session for members to work on personal challenges.
6. **Action step**: A clear commitment each member makes before the next session.
7. **A post-session worksheet**: Reflection or planning exercise to complete before the next meeting.

### Cohort-Based Model

Cohort-based programs start and end on fixed dates with a specific group of members. This creates:

- **Scarcity**: The program runs on a schedule; joining is time-limited.
- **Community cohesion**: The group bonds over the shared timeline.
- **Clear container**: Everyone knows when it starts and ends.
- **Launch rhythm**: You market the program, open enrollment, run the cohort, then prepare for the next one.

CoachSheets supports this model well: create a program with sequential steps, each step containing a worksheet and a scheduled send date aligned to your cohort calendar.

## Using CoachSheets Groups and Assignments

CoachSheets provides specific features for group coaching delivery.

### Groups Feature

The Groups feature allows you to organize clients into cohorts. A group represents one cohort of your program. Within a group you can:

- View all members of a specific cohort
- Assign worksheets to the entire group at once
- Track completion status per member per worksheet

### Assignments for Groups

When creating an assignment in CoachSheets, you can select a group as the recipient. The assignment is then sent to every client in that group simultaneously. This is ideal for:

- Sending pre-session preparation worksheets to all cohort members
- Distributing between-session reflection exercises
- Sending program completion assessments

### Program Enrollments

CoachSheets' program enrollment feature is the most powerful tool for cohort-based programs. You define a program with steps (each step = one week), each step has a worksheet, and the program automatically delivers worksheets on the schedule you set. When a new cohort starts, you enroll all group members in the program and the system handles the rest.

## Mastermind vs Group Coaching vs Workshop

These three formats are often confused. Each serves a different purpose.

### Group Coaching

The coach leads the session with a structured curriculum. The coach provides teaching, facilitates exercises, and delivers individual coaching within the group. The coach is the expert and facilitator.

**Best for**: Clients who want structured learning and accountability within a community.

### Mastermind

The group members coach each other. The facilitator's role is to guide the conversation, ask powerful questions, and maintain a safe container, but the primary value comes from peer input and collective wisdom. Members share challenges and receive feedback from the group.

**Best for**: Experienced clients who have foundational knowledge and need peer accountability and diverse perspectives.

### Workshop

A workshop is a single-session (or multi-session) teaching event. The coach delivers content, exercises, and takeaways in a defined period. There is no ongoing coaching relationship; participants attend, learn, and leave.

**Best for**: Lead generation, introductory experiences, or teaching a specific skill.

### How to Combine Them

A strong practice often uses all three:

1. Run free or low-cost workshops to attract new leads.
2. Convert workshop attendees into a cohort-based group coaching program.
3. Offer a mastermind as a premium tier for graduates of the group program.

This creates a ladder: workshop → group program → mastermind → 1:1 premium coaching.

## Pricing Group Programs

Group programs should be priced significantly lower than 1:1 coaching but high enough that members are invested.

| Format | Typical Price Range | Per-Participant Revenue |
|--------|--------------------|------------------------|
| 4-week workshop series | $100 - $300 | $100 - $300 |
| 8-week group program | $500 - $1,500 | $500 - $1,500 |
| 3-month mastermind | $1,000 - $3,000 | $1,000 - $3,000 |
| Ongoing membership | $100 - $500/month | Ongoing |

At 10 members per cohort, an 8-week program at $750 each generates $7,500. That is equivalent to 3-4 premium 1:1 clients but requires less total time.
