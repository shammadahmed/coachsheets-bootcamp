# Project: Coaching Philosophy Statement

> **Type:** Project
> **Estimated time:** 2--3 hours (writing + self-assessment)
> **Word count target:** 400--600 words
> **Submission:** Capstone portfolio component

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this project, you should be able to:
- Articulate your personal definition of coaching in your own words, distinct from institutional definitions
- Connect at least two ICF Core Competencies to your specific coaching approach
- Define your ethical boundaries with clarity and specificity
- State your position on the client's autonomy, resourcefulness, and agency in the coaching relationship
- Produce a written philosophy statement that reflects your authentic voice and serves as a foundational document for your practice

---

## Project Brief

A coaching philosophy statement is to your practice what a mission statement is to an organization. It defines who you are as a coach, what you believe about human potential, and how you approach the transformational relationship between coach and client. It is not a marketing document -- it is an internal compass.

In this project, you will write a **400--600 word coaching philosophy statement** that articulates your approach, your values, and your ethical boundaries. This document will become part of your capstone portfolio and will serve as a touchstone throughout your coaching career.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Many experienced coaches report revisiting and revising their philosophy statement annually. This is not a one-time exercise. What you write today will evolve as you grow. The goal is not perfection -- it is clarity and authenticity.

---

## Why Write a Coaching Philosophy Statement?

New coaches often borrow language from their training program, from the ICF, or from coaches they admire. That is a useful starting point, but it is not a philosophy. A philosophy statement forces you to answer fundamental questions:

- **What do I actually believe** about human beings and their capacity for change?
- **What is my role** in the coaching relationship?
- **Where are my non-negotiables** -- the lines I will not cross?
- **Why do I do this work** in the first place?
- **How do I hold the tension** between supporting a client and challenging them?

Clients will not read your philosophy statement. But your philosophy will show up in every session -- in the questions you ask, the silences you hold, the boundaries you maintain, and the way you respond when a client asks for advice. Writing it down makes those implicit beliefs explicit, which makes you a more intentional and consistent coach.

## Requirements

Your philosophy statement must address all five of the following areas. Each requirement includes guiding prompts to help you write.

### 1. Define What Coaching Means to You (in Your Own Words)

Do not copy the ICF definition. Write your own. Your definition should reflect your personality, your worldview, and the kind of coaching you want to practice.

Guiding prompts:
- What is coaching, at its essence? What happens in a coaching conversation that does not happen in any other kind of conversation?
- What metaphors or images come to mind when you think about coaching? (e.g., "Coaching is like being a mirror," "Coaching is like walking alongside someone in the dark.")
- Who is coaching for, and what makes someone ready for it?

Example (do not copy -- write your own):
> "To me, coaching is a structured conversation that helps people see what they have been unable to see on their own, so they can choose what they have been unable to choose on their own. It is not advice, not therapy, not teaching -- it is a unique partnership built on trust, curiosity, and the unwavering belief that the client already holds the answers they are looking for."

### 2. Reference at Least 2 ICF Core Competencies

Choose competencies that genuinely resonate with your approach. Explain how each one shows up in your coaching practice.

The eight ICF Core Competencies are:
1. Demonstrates Ethical Practice
2. Embodies a Coaching Mindset
3. Establishes and Maintains Agreements
4. Cultivates Trust and Safety
5. Maintains Presence
6. Listens Actively
7. Evokes Awareness
8. Facilitates Client Growth

Full descriptions: [ICF Core Competencies](https://coachingfederation.org/core-competencies)

Guiding prompts:
- Which two competencies feel most natural to you?
- What does that competency look like in a real session? Give a concrete example.
- How does this competency connect to your values or personal history?

Example:
> "Competency 6 -- Listens Actively -- is the foundation of my practice. I believe the deepest coaching happens when I stop thinking about what to ask next and simply receive what the client is offering. When I listen at this level, I hear not just the client's words but the gaps between them -- the hesitations, the shifts in tone, the places where the energy drops or rises. That is where the coaching lives."

### 3. State Your Ethical Boundaries

Be specific about what you will and will not do as a coach. Include the coaching vs. therapy boundary and the conditions under which you would refer a client out.

Guiding prompts:
- Where is the line between coaching and therapy in your practice?
- Under what circumstances would you refer a client to another professional?
- How will you handle a client who asks for advice?
- What will you do if a client discloses a clinical concern (trauma, self-harm, active mental health crisis)?

> [!IMPORTANT]
- The ICF Code of Ethics requires coaches to "recognize that the client understands the inherent value of therapy and refer them to such services as appropriate." Your philosophy statement should demonstrate that you take this obligation seriously.

### 4. Describe Your Stance on Client Autonomy and Resourcefulness

Coaching rests on the radical assumption that the client is naturally creative, resourceful, and whole. What does this mean to you? How does it affect what you do in sessions?

Guiding prompts:
- Do you believe every client has the capacity to solve their own challenges? What if they do not believe it themselves?
- How do you hold the tension between supporting a client and letting them struggle productively?
- What is your role when a client feels stuck or hopeless?

### 5. Include Something Personal

Why do you coach? What draws you to this work? This is not about your resume -- it is about your motivation and your connection to this practice.

Guiding prompts:
- What experience or insight led you to coaching?
- What do you most love about coaching conversations?
- What is the one thing you want every client to know about you as their coach?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A philosophy statement without a personal dimension reads like a textbook. Your clients will not read this document, but your authentic voice in it will shape how you show up. Write something that is true, not something that sounds professional.

## Rubric

Use this rubric to self-assess your statement before submission.

| Criterion | Meets Expectations | Does Not Meet |
|---|---|---|
| **References >=2 ICF competencies** | At least two competencies are named and clearly connected to the coach's specific approach with concrete examples or reasoning | Fewer than two competencies; competencies named but not connected to the coach's approach; generic description without personal application |
| **Ethical boundary stated** | Coaching vs. therapy distinction is clearly defined; specific conditions for referral are named; boundaries around advice-giving are addressed | No mention of coaching vs. therapy boundary; referral conditions are vague or absent; ethical boundaries are implied rather than stated |
| **Personal voice present** | The writing reflects an individual perspective; language feels authentic rather than generic; reader gains a sense of who this coach is as a person | Language reads like a training manual or institutional copy; no personal motivation, story, or distinctive perspective; could belong to any coach |
| **400--600 words** | Statement falls within the target range | Under 350 words (too brief to meet all requirements) or over 700 words (lacks focus) |
| **Original coaching definition** | Coaching is defined in the coach's own words using original metaphors, language, or framing | Definition is copied or closely paraphrased from ICF or another source without personal reinterpretation |
| **Autonomy and resourcefulness addressed** | The coach's stance on the client's creative capacity and agency is clearly articulated; tension between support and challenge is addressed | No mention of client autonomy; resourcefulness assumption is absent; coach's stance on the client's role is unclear |

## Submission Instructions

1. **Write your statement** (400--600 words) in a document. Use plain text or Markdown format.

2. **Save the final version** to your bootcamp portfolio folder. You will submit it as part of the final capstone portfolio at the end of this program.

3. **Self-assess** against the rubric above. Be honest. If you do not meet a criterion, revise before submitting.

4. **Peer review (optional but recommended):** Share your statement with one peer from this bootcamp. Exchange feedback on:
   - Does the statement sound like the person who wrote it? (Voice)
   - Would you trust this coach with a client? (Ethics and authenticity)
   - What is missing or unclear? (Completeness)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Do not skip the peer review step just because it is optional. One of the most common reflections from coaches who complete this project is: "Reading someone else's philosophy made me realize what was missing from my own." Peer review is as much about your learning as about theirs.

## Example Excerpts (for Inspiration Only)

These are excerpts from real coaching philosophy statements. Use them to spark your thinking, not as templates to copy.

> "I believe every client arrives at coaching already whole. My job is not to fix them -- it is to help them see what they have stopped seeing. Often that is their own strength."

> "The question I return to most often is: 'What do you want, and what are you willing to risk to get it?' I hold that question lightly but persistently."

> "I will not give you advice. I will not tell you what to do. What I will do is stand beside you while you figure it out -- and I will ask the questions you have been avoiding."

## Assignment

1. **Write your first draft.** Set a timer for 45 minutes. Write without editing. Do not worry about word count or polish. Just get your thoughts on the page.

2. **Revise against the rubric.** Wait at least 24 hours. Re-read your draft and assess it against all six rubric criteria. Revise until you meet each criterion.

3. **Peer review.** Exchange statements with a peer. Provide written feedback on voice, ethics, and completeness. Receive and incorporate feedback.

4. **Publish your final version.** Save the final statement to your bootcamp portfolio. You will reference it in the capstone.
