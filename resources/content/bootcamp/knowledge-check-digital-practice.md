## Scenario Questions

### Question 1: Worksheet Completion Resistance

**Scenario:** You have been coaching Priya for three weeks. She attends sessions enthusiastically but has not completed a single between-session worksheet. When you ask about it, she says, "I just forget. I get busy and then the due date passes." You have been sending a single reminder one day before the due date.

**Question:** Using the BJ Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP), analyze Priya's situation. Which element(s) might be the bottleneck, and what specific changes would you make to your reminder strategy?

---

### Question 2: Data Privacy for an EU Client

**Scenario:** You are a coach based in the United States. A client from Spain enrolls in your 12-week coaching program. You use a US-based digital coaching platform. The client asks, "How is my data protected under European privacy laws?"

**Question:** Does GDPR apply in this situation? What specific information should you share with the client about their data protection rights?

---

### Question 3: Element Type Selection

**Scenario:** Your client Marcus wants to understand why he keeps procrastinating on important career goals. He is intellectually aware of the problem but has not made progress despite six sessions. You want a worksheet that helps him surface the underlying beliefs driving his procrastination.

**Question:** Which worksheet element type(s) would you use for this situation? Justify your choice with reference to the element-matching principles from Lesson 3.2.

---

### Question 4: Nudge Tone Audit

**Scenario:** One of your peers sends this reminder to their client: "Just a reminder that our agreement clearly states worksheets must be completed 24 hours before our session. Failure to complete worksheets impacts the quality of our coaching. Please prioritize this."

**Question:** Identify the problems with this reminder's tone. Rewrite it using the encouraging (not nagging) principles from Lesson 3.5.

---

### Question 5: AI Summary Ethics

**Scenario:** You use an AI tool that generates session summaries based on worksheet answers and session notes. The AI produces a summary stating: "Client shows signs of generalized anxiety disorder and would benefit from clinical intervention." You know the client has never mentioned anxiety and has not sought a diagnosis.

**Question:** How should you respond to this AI output? What are the ethical issues at play, referencing the principles from Lesson 3.6?

---

### Question 6: Program Design for a 6-Week Engagement

**Scenario:** You are designing a 6-week coaching program for clients working on career transitions. The engagement includes weekly 45-minute sessions. You want worksheets to arrive 48 hours before each session so you have time to review them.

**Question:** Design the program structure. Which cadence (fixed_offset or fixed_weekly) would you use? What release day and due offset would you configure? Explain your reasoning.

---

### Question 7: Progressive Disclosure Audit

**Scenario:** A worksheet in your library has the following questions in order:
1. "Write about your childhood relationship with your parents." (textarea)
2. "What is one goal you want to achieve this month?" (textarea)
3. "Rate your energy level." (scale, 1-10)

**Question:** What is wrong with this question order from a progressive disclosure perspective? Reorder the questions appropriately and explain why your order is better.

---

### Question 8: Team and Multi-Coach Ethics

**Scenario:** You are part of a coaching practice with three other coaches sharing one CoachSheets account (using the Teams feature). One coach wants to invite their client to join the platform. Another coach suggests disabling encryption to make data "easier to access" across the team.

**Question:** What are the data privacy concerns with sharing a platform account across multiple coaches? How should you respond to the suggestion about disabling encryption?

---

### Question 9: Reminder Frequency Adjustment

**Scenario:** A new client named Alex has completed worksheets consistently for six weeks without any reminders. In week 7, Alex travels internationally for work and misses a worksheet deadline. Alex messages you: "So sorry, the time zones got me. I will complete it tonight."

**Question:** Should you adjust your reminder strategy for Alex? If so, how? Consider both the short-term situation (the missed worksheet) and the long-term engagement pattern.

---

### Question 10: Worksheet Question Count

**Scenario:** You are designing a pre-session check-in worksheet. You have drafted 18 questions covering energy, mood, progress on goals, obstacles, wins, values alignment, relationships, health, finances, and plans for the coming week. The worksheet takes an estimated 35 minutes to complete.

**Question:** Apply the worksheet design principles from Lesson 3.2. How many questions would you cut, and which criteria would you use to decide what stays and what goes?

---

### Question 11: Automation Rule Configuration

**Scenario:** You want to automatically send an intake worksheet to every new client immediately after their first session is scheduled. You want the worksheet to have a due date of 3 days after it is sent, and you want to send a reminder 1 day before the due date.

**Question:** Walk through the automation rule setup in CoachSheets. What trigger would you select, what timing offsets would you configure, and what due offset would you set?

---

### Question 12: Client Consent Documentation

**Scenario:** A new client completes your intake worksheet, which includes a consent statement at the bottom: "By completing this worksheet, you agree to our data practices." The client checks the box and submits. Three weeks later, the client asks: "What exactly did I agree to regarding my data?"

**Question:** What is missing from this consent process? Rewrite it following best practices from Lesson 3.6, including specific elements that should be present in a valid consent statement.

## Answer Key

### Answer 1

Priya likely has sufficient motivation (she attends enthusiastically) but the prompt is insufficient. A single reminder one day before is too weak for someone who struggles with time management. Apply B=MAP: increase Ability by ensuring the worksheet link is in her calendar or a recurring notification; strengthen the Prompt by adding a three-phase sequence (preview 2 days before, gentle nudge 1 day before, support check 1 day after). Also consider asking Priya what time of day she prefers to receive reminders.

### Answer 2

Yes, GDPR applies because you have a client in the EU, regardless of your location. You should inform the client of their rights: right to access their data, right to erasure, right to data portability. You should confirm that your platform encrypts data in transit and at rest, provide a copy of your Data Processing Agreement, and ensure the client knows they can request deletion at any time.

### Answer 3

Use a Thought Record element combined with Cognitive Distortions. The Thought Record helps Marcus identify the specific thoughts that arise when he considers working on his career goals. The Cognitive Distortions element helps him see which thinking patterns (catastrophizing, all-or-nothing, mind-reading) are at play. These elements are designed for surfacing unconscious beliefs, unlike a textarea which would require Marcus to self-identify patterns without a framework.

### Answer 4

Problems: The tone is guilt-inducing ("our agreement clearly states"), shaming ("Failure to complete worksheets"), and demanding ("Please prioritize this"). It positions the coach as an authority figure enforcing rules rather than a partner. Rewrite: "Hi [Client], I noticed the worksheet is still open. I know life gets busy. Our next session will be richest if I can review your answers beforehand. Would a 24-hour extension help, or is there something about the questions you would like to discuss? I am here either way."

### Answer 5

This is a NEVER-APPROPRIATE use of AI. The AI is making a clinical diagnosis, which is outside the scope of both coaching and appropriate AI use. The coach should: (1) NOT share the AI's diagnosis with the client; (2) adjust the AI prompt or settings to prevent diagnostic language; (3) document the incident as an AI error; (4) use professional judgment to determine whether the client actually shows signs needing clinical referral (based on the coach's observations, not the AI's output). The ICF Code of Ethics requires coaches to work within their competence and refer to appropriate professionals when needed.

### Answer 6

Use fixed_weekly cadence. Set worksheets to release every Monday at 9:00 AM (48 hours before a Wednesday session). Set the due offset to 1 day (due Tuesday at 9:00 AM, 24 hours before session). Fixed weekly works best because sessions are on a regular weekly schedule. The 48-hour gap gives the client Sunday evening + Monday to complete, and Monday evening for coach review. Worksheet steps: Week 1 (Career Vision), Week 2 (Skills Assessment), Week 3 (Network Map), Week 4 (Action Plan), Week 5 (Obstacle Exploration), Week 6 (Commitment and Next Steps).

### Answer 7

The order violates progressive disclosure. Question 1 asks about a potentially vulnerable childhood topic with zero warm-up -- the hardest question first. Question 3 (energy scale) is the easiest and should be first. Correct order: (3) Rate your energy level (low-friction scale), (2) What is one goal you want to achieve this month? (moderate reflection), (1) Childhood relationship exploration (deep reflection, only after trust is established through preceding questions). This mirrors how a coach would naturally sequence a conversation.

### Answer 8

Multiple coaches sharing one account means clients cannot be sure which coach has access to their data. This violates transparency and may breach consent agreements. The suggestion to disable encryption is dangerous and unethical. Response: (1) Use proper team accounts with role-based access so each coach sees only their own clients. (2) Encryption should never be disabled -- it is the minimum standard for client data protection. (3) Review your platform's team features to ensure proper data isolation between coaches.

### Answer 9

Short term: Send a supportive message acknowledging the time zone issue. Do not add permanent reminders for a client whose normal pattern is consistent. Long term: For international travel periods, proactively ask Alex if they would like a time zone-adjusted reminder or a pre-travel worksheet. The three-phase reminder strategy is a default; individual clients may need customized approaches. Alex has earned the trust of consistent completion -- do not over-correct for one outlier event.

### Answer 10

Cut from 18 to 8-10 questions. Use these criteria: (1) Relevance to the upcoming session topic -- only include questions directly connected to what you will discuss. (2) Unique contribution -- if two questions measure similar things, keep the stronger one. (3) Client energy -- a 35-minute pre-session worksheet is too long and will reduce completion rates. (4) Actionability -- every question should produce information you will use in the session. Recommended 10 minutes maximum for a pre-session check-in.

### Answer 11

Trigger type: "When a session is completed" (or "When a client is created" if you want it immediately after intake). Set the timing type to "Send immediately" or offset 0. Set the due offset to 3 days (72 hours). For the reminder, create a second automation rule with timing offset of -1440 minutes (1 day before the due date). This means: Worksheet is sent immediately, due in 3 days, reminder sent 2 days after release (1 day before due date).

### Answer 12

The consent statement is inadequate because: (1) It is buried at the bottom of a worksheet rather than presented clearly. (2) It does not specify what data is collected, how it is stored, or how it is used. (3) It does not mention AI use. (4) It does not inform the client of their rights. (5) "By completing this worksheet, you agree" is passive consent -- it does not require affirmative opt-in. A valid consent statement should include: what data is collected, the purpose of collection, whether AI is used and how, the client's rights (access, deletion, portability), how to opt out of AI features, and a contact for privacy questions. It should require an explicit checkbox separate from the worksheet submission.
