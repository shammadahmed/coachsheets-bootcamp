## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this knowledge check, you should be able to:
- Discriminate between the six coaching frameworks covered in Module 2 (GROW, MI, CBC, SFBC, AI, Values-Based, Narrative).
- Select the most appropriate framework for a given client scenario.
- Justify your framework choice based on the client's language, presenting problem, and readiness for change.

# Module 2: Framework Knowledge Check

This knowledge check contains 15 scenario-based questions covering all six coaching frameworks from Module 2. Questions are a mix of multiple choice and short answer. Complete all questions, then check your answers at the end.

---

## Questions

### Question 1 (Multiple Choice)

A client says: "I know I should exercise more, but I keep finding excuses. Part of me wants to change and part of me does not. I have been going back and forth for months."

Which framework would you start with, and why?

A) GROW — The client needs a clear goal and action plan.
B) Motivational Interviewing — The client is ambivalent and stuck in contemplation.
C) CBC — The client needs to examine the thoughts behind the excuses.
D) SFBC — The client needs to imagine what life would be like if they exercised.

---

### Question 2 (Short Answer)

A client says: "I am just not a public speaker. Every time I have to present, my mind goes blank. I have always been this way."

Identify the cognitive distortion(s) at play. Which framework would you use, and what might your first intervention be?

---

### Question 3 (Multiple Choice)

A client who recently started therapy for depression tells you: "My therapist says I am making great progress. I would like to work with you on building a daily routine that supports my recovery."

What is the most ethically appropriate response?

A) Begin coaching immediately using GROW to structure the routine-building.
B) Decline coaching because the client has a clinical condition.
C) Confirm with the client and their therapist that coaching is appropriate as an adjunct, then proceed with scope-appropriate work.
D) Use CBC to address the thinking patterns related to depression.

### Question 4 (Short Answer)

### Question 13 (Multiple Choice)

### Question 14 (Short Answer)

### Question 15 (Multiple Choice)

Which of the following statements best describes the relationship between coaching frameworks?

A) Coaches should master one framework and use it exclusively for consistency.
B) Frameworks are interchangeable — pick one at random for each session.
C) Effective coaches learn multiple frameworks and select based on the client's presenting situation, stage of change, and preferred working style.
D) Frameworks are only useful for ICF-credentialed coaches.

---

## Answer Key

### Answer 1
**B) Motivational Interviewing.** The client is in Contemplation — aware of the need for change but ambivalent. Pushing for a goal (GROW) or solution (SFBC) would likely increase resistance. MI meets the client where they are and explores the ambivalence.

### Answer 2
**Cognitive distortions:** Labeling ("I am not a public speaker"), Overgeneralization ("every time"), Fortune-telling ("my mind goes blank").
**Framework:** CBC — use a thought record to examine the automatic thought and find evidence that contradicts it. For example: "Has there ever been a time when you gave a presentation that went better than expected? What was different about that time?"

### Answer 3
**C) Confirm with the client and their therapist that coaching is appropriate, then proceed with scope-appropriate work (building routines).** Coaching can coexist with therapy when boundaries are clear. CBC would be inappropriate here because the client has depression.

### Answer 4
**Appreciative Inquiry** is the best fit. The team is stuck in a deficit narrative. First question: "Tell me about a time when this team was at its best. What made that possible?" This shifts focus to strengths and peak experiences.

### Answer 5
**C) Values-Based Coaching.** This is a classic values misalignment presentation. The client achieved external goals that do not align with their core values. GROW would miss the underlying issue.

### Answer 6
**Cognitive distortions:** Mind-reading ("everyone thinks I am incompetent"), Emotional reasoning ("I feel them watching me").
**Coach response:** "Let us separate what you know for certain from what you are assuming. What evidence do you have that they think you are incompetent? Is there another possible explanation for their behavior?"

### Answer 7
**D) Both B and C are valid starting points.** The client is in an exploratory, open-ended space. AI helps surface what energizes them (Discover/Dream). Narrative coaching helps deconstruct the "lost" story and find alternative narratives.

### Answer 8
Use the Miracle Question to shift the focus from problem to solution: "If a miracle happened tonight and all these work problems were resolved, what would be the first small sign you noticed tomorrow morning?" Then scale: "Where are you relative to that miracle today?" And find exceptions: "When is the problem slightly less intense?"

### Answer 9
**B) CBC + Narrative.** CBC helps the client identify and challenge the cognitive distortion (imposter syndrome = disqualifying the positive + mind-reading). Narrative coaching helps them re-author their identity from "fraud" to "someone who earned their promotion and is still learning."

### Answer 10
Several frameworks could work:
- **GROW:** "What would the ideal outcome of this decision look like?"
- **Values-Based:** "What values are in conflict here? Which one matters more to you?"
- **Decision matrix (CBC):** "What are the pros and cons of each option? What fears are driving each side?"

### Answer 11
**D) All of the above could be relevant.** MI could explore whether the client truly wants the goals they set. CBC could uncover the thoughts that arise at the moment of action. GROW could strengthen the Will stage. The best coach would explore first, then choose.

### Answer 12
Externalized response: "It sounds like 'giving up' has been showing up whenever you get about a week into a new habit. Let us talk about that pattern as something outside of you. When does 'giving up' usually arrive? What does it say to you? And when have you been able to extend past the one-week mark?"

### Answer 13
**C) Explore the resistance.** The client is giving the coach important feedback. GROW is a tool, not a straitjacket. "Happiness" as a goal may need unpacking — and the client's frustration may point to a values-based concern rather than a poorly-defined goal.

### Answer 14
**Values-Based Coaching** is the primary framework. Questions:
- "You clearly value both career and family deeply. What would it look like to define success differently — not as 100% to both, but as 'enough' to both?"
- **SFBC** also works: "If you woke up tomorrow and this conflict felt manageable, what would be different?"
- **CBC** could help: "What thoughts arise when you say you are 'failing' at both? Are those thoughts fully accurate?"

### Answer 15
**C)** Effective coaches learn multiple frameworks and select based on the client's presenting situation, stage of change, and preferred working style. Framework fluency gives the coach flexibility; framework rigidity limits their effectiveness.
