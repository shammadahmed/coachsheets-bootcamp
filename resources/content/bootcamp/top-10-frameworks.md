Successful coaching relies on structure. Without a framework, coaching sessions can easily devolve into unstructured venting sessions. By anchoring your practice in proven methodologies, you provide consistent, measurable results for your clients. Here are the Top 10 Frameworks every modern coach should know.

---

### 1. GROW Model
*Goal, Reality, Options, Will*
The undisputed gold standard of coaching models. It provides a simple, logical sequence for problem-solving and goal setting.
- **Goal:** What do you want to achieve?
- **Reality:** Where are you right now?
- **Options:** What could you do?
- **Will:** What will you do?

### 2. CLEAR Model
*Contract, Listen, Explore, Action, Review*
Ideal for management and executive coaching, focusing heavily on the initial agreement and subsequent review.
- **Contract:** Establish the scope and desired outcome of the session.
- **Listen:** Active listening to understand the client's perspective.
- **Explore:** Probe deeper into the emotional and factual realities.
- **Action:** Commit to specific next steps.
- **Review:** Audit progress in subsequent sessions.

### 3. OSCAR Model
*Outcome, Scale, Know-how, Affirm & Action, Review*
A highly solution-focused framework that incorporates scaling (rating progress 1-10) to quantify subjective feelings.
- **Scale:** On a scale of 1-10, how close are you to the outcome?
- **Know-how:** What skills do you already possess that can help?

### 4. WOOP Model
*Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan*
Developed by Gabriele Oettingen, WOOP is grounded in the science of "mental contrasting." It forces clients to confront the obstacles rather than just fantasizing about the outcome.
- **Obstacle:** What is the primary internal obstacle stopping you?
- **Plan:** If [Obstacle] occurs, then I will [Action].

### 5. STEPP Model
*Subject, Target, Emotion, Perception, Plan*
A deeply emotionally-aware framework. It addresses how the client *feels* about the target before moving to the plan, making it excellent for navigating imposter syndrome or anxiety.

### 6. TGROW Model
*Topic + Goal, Reality, Options, Will*
A slight variation of the GROW model that explicitly separates the overarching "Topic" (e.g., Career Transition) from the specific "Goal" of the current session (e.g., Update Resume).

### 7. Solution-Focused Brief Coaching (SFBC)
This approach ignores the root cause of the problem entirely and focuses exclusively on the solution. It relies heavily on the "Miracle Question": *If you woke up tomorrow and the problem was solved, what would be the first thing you noticed?*

### 8. Appreciative Inquiry (4-D Cycle)
*Discover, Dream, Design, Destiny*
A strengths-based approach used heavily in organizational coaching. Instead of asking "What is broken?", it asks "What is working well, and how can we do more of it?"

### 9. Values-Based Coaching
Focuses on aligning a client's actions with their core values. When clients feel "stuck" or unfulfilled despite outward success, it is almost always due to a values misalignment.

### 10. Cognitive Behavioral Coaching (CBC)
*(Non-Clinical)*
Adapted from CBT, CBC focuses on identifying and reframing limiting beliefs. It operates on the premise that our *thoughts* dictate our *feelings*, which dictate our *actions*.
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Ethical Boundary:** While derived from clinical practices, CBC must only be used to overcome professional or personal blockers (e.g., public speaking anxiety) and never to treat clinical depression or anxiety disorders.
