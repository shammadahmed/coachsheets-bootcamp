By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Identify which competencies you already demonstrate and which you need to develop.
- Understand how the competencies map to a typical coaching session.

## Why the ICF Core Competencies Matter

The International Coaching Federation (ICF) is the largest global coaching organization, with over 50,000 members across 140+ countries. Their Core Competencies were updated in 2019 to reflect current coaching practice and research. Whether or not you pursue ICF credentialing (ACC, PCC, MCC), these competencies are the gold standard for what competent coaching looks like.

Think of them as a map. They tell you what good coaching *looks like*, so you can assess your own work honestly and know what to improve.

The 8 competencies are grouped into 4 domains:

- **Domain 1: Foundation** (Competencies 1-2) — Who you are as a coach
- **Domain 2: Co-creating the Relationship** (Competencies 3-4) — How you show up with the client
- **Domain 3: Communicating Effectively** (Competencies 5-7) — What you do during the session
- **Domain 4: Cultivating Learning and Growth** (Competency 8) — How you help the client move forward

## The 8 Core Competencies

### 1. Demonstrates Ethical Practice
Understands and applies ICF Ethical Standards to every interaction. This means maintaining confidentiality, clarifying roles and boundaries, recognizing when coaching isn't the right fit, and referring clients to other professionals when needed. This competency underpins everything else — without ethics, the rest is technique without integrity.

### 2. Embodies a Coaching Mindset
The coach maintains a mindset that is open, curious, flexible, and client-centered. This is about *who you are* as much as *what you do*. It includes: being present, managing your own reactions, seeing the client as resourceful and whole, and committing to your own ongoing development. This competency is the hardest to assess in yourself — it requires self-awareness and often supervision or peer coaching to develop.

### 3. Establishes and Maintains Agreements
Partners with the client to create a clear coaching agreement — both for the overall engagement and for each individual session. What does the client want to achieve? What does success look like? What is the scope of the coaching relationship? This competency prevents the most common coaching failure: meandering sessions where neither coach nor client knows what they're working toward.

### 4. Cultivates Trust and Safety
Creates a safe, supportive environment where the client feels respected, accepted, and free to explore honestly. This includes: confidentiality, non-judgment, permission to be vulnerable, and cultural sensitivity. Trust is built over time through consistency — every interaction either strengthens or erodes it.

### 5. Maintains Awareness
Is fully present and attentive during the coaching session. Notices not just what the client says, but how they say it — tone, energy, body language, what they avoid, what they repeat. Maintains awareness of the client's goals for the session while staying flexible enough to follow what emerges. This is where coaching moves from technique to art.

### 6. Listens Actively
Listens not just to words but to meaning, emotion, and context. Pays attention to what is said and what is left unsaid. Reflects and paraphrases to confirm understanding. Notices themes and patterns across sessions. Active listening in coaching is deeper than everyday listening — it is a disciplined attention that most people have never experienced from another person.

### 7. Evokes Awareness
Asks powerful questions that help the client see things from new perspectives. Challenges the client's assumptions with respect. Uses intuition, observation, and direct communication to surface insights the client hasn't reached alone. This is the competency that most clearly distinguishes coaching from conversation — the coach is *actively working* to expand the client's awareness, not just listening supportively.

### 8. Facilitates Client Growth
Partners with the client to turn insights into action. Helps the client define concrete steps, identify potential obstacles, and commit to next actions. Acknowledges and celebrates progress. Manages accountability without judgment. This competency closes the loop — awareness without action is incomplete coaching.

## How the Competencies Map to a Session

A typical coaching session touches all 8 competencies, though not in a strict order:

1. **Ethical Practice** — present throughout (confidentiality, boundaries, scope)
2. **Coaching Mindset** — present throughout (staying present, non-judgmental)
3. **Agreement** — opening ("What would make this session valuable for you today?")
4. **Trust & Safety** — built through tone, presence, and consistency
5. **Maintains Awareness** — throughout (noticing energy shifts, patterns)
6. **Listens Actively** — throughout (reflecting, paraphrasing, noticing themes)
7. **Evokes Awareness** — the middle of the session (questions, challenges, observations)
8. **Facilitates Growth** — the closing (action steps, commitments, accountability)
