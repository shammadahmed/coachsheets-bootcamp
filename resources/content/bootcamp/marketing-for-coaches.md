# Lesson 4: Marketing for Coaches

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- Build a content marketing strategy tailored to your coaching niche
- Use discovery calls as a primary conversion mechanism
- Develop a referral network and cultivate word-of-mouth growth
- Identify why most coach marketing fails and how to avoid those pitfalls

---

## Content Marketing for Coaches

Content marketing is the most sustainable way to attract coaching clients. Unlike paid advertising, which stops working the moment you stop paying, content compounds over time. A blog post you write today can generate inquiries years from now.

### Blogging

A blog is your home base. Every piece of content you create elsewhere (social media, podcasts, guest posts) should drive back to your blog. Effective blog content for coaches includes:

- **Problem-focused posts**: "Three Signs You Are Stuck in a Career That Does Not Fit You"
- **Process posts**: "What Happens in a Coaching Discovery Call"
- **Case studies** (anonymized): "How Sarah Went From Burnout to Purpose in 90 Days"
- **Resource posts**: "Five Worksheets That Will Clarify Your Life Goals"

Write 1-2 blog posts per month minimum. Consistency matters more than frequency. A single high-quality post each month for a year creates a library of 12 pieces that work for you around the clock.

### Social Media

Do not try to be on every platform. Pick one or two where your ideal client spends time and go deep.

- **LinkedIn**: Best for executive, career, and leadership coaches. Publish long-form posts and engage in industry groups.
- **Instagram**: Best for wellness, mindset, and lifestyle coaches. Use Reels and carousel posts with actionable advice.
- **YouTube**: Best for coaches who can demonstrate their process visually. Session demonstrations, worksheet walkthroughs, and Q&A videos perform well.
- **TikTok**: Best for reaching younger audiences with short, punchy content.

The golden rule of social media for coaches: give value first, ask for the call second. Your content should teach something useful in every post. When prospects learn from you, they trust you.

### Podcasting

Either start your own podcast or be a guest on others. Podcasting builds deep trust because listeners hear your voice, your pace, and your thinking over 30-60 minutes. That is far more intimacy than a blog post or social media caption can provide.

If you are new, prioritise being a guest on established podcasts in your niche. A single podcast appearance can drive more qualified leads than months of social media posting.

## Discovery Calls as Marketing

Your discovery call is not just an intake step — it is your most powerful marketing channel. A well-run discovery call converts at 50-80% for experienced coaches. Here is the structure:

1. **Connect (5 minutes)**: Build rapport. Ask about their week, their context, why they reached out now.

2. **Explore (10 minutes)**: Understand their situation. Ask open-ended questions:
   - What is the biggest challenge you are facing right now?
   - What have you tried so far?
   - What would it mean to you if this were resolved?

3. **Reframe (5 minutes)**: Reflect back what you heard. Name the pattern. Offer a perspective they may not have considered. This is where you demonstrate your coaching value live.

4. **Offer (5 minutes)**: Explain how your package addresses their specific situation. Be direct about price and commitment.

5. **Decide (5 minutes)**: Ask if they want to move forward. If yes, send the agreement. If no or not yet, ask what they need and leave the door open.

The discovery call is a marketing conversation disguised as a coaching conversation. Your goal is not to "sell" — it is to show the client what is possible with your help.

## Referral Networks

Referrals are the highest-quality leads you will ever get. A referred client already trusts you because someone they trust vouched for you. Build referral networks intentionally:

### Cross-Referral Partnerships

Identify professionals who serve your ideal client but are not in your exact niche. For example:

- A career coach could partner with a resume writer and a recruiter
- A wellness coach could partner with a nutritionist and a physiotherapist
- An ADHD coach could partner with a psychiatrist and an occupational therapist

Set up a mutual referral agreement. Send them clients when appropriate, and they will send clients to you.

### Client Referral Programs

Satisfied clients are your best marketers. Make it easy for them to refer others:

- Ask directly at the end of a successful engagement: "If you know anyone who might benefit from this work, I would love an introduction."
- Offer a referral incentive: one free session for every paid client they refer.
- Provide a shareable one-page PDF that explains what you do and who you help.

Most coaches never ask for referrals. The ones who do grow faster.

## The "Give Value First" Approach

The most effective marketing strategy for coaches is simple: give away your best work for free.

When you publish a high-quality worksheet, write a detailed blog post, or record a guided exercise, you are not giving away your services. You are demonstrating your expertise and building trust. A prospect who completes one of your worksheets and experiences a genuine insight is far more likely to pay for a full coaching engagement.

Examples of "give value first" content:

- Free downloadable worksheet (hosted via CoachSheets magic link)
- Email course: "7 Days to Career Clarity"
- Live workshop or webinar (recorded for later viewing)
- Free discovery session (not a sales call, a real coaching session)

The paradox is that coaches who give the most away attract the most paying clients.

## Why Most Coach Marketing Fails

The coaching industry is crowded. Most coach marketing fails for the same reasons:

1. **Too generic**: "I help people live their best lives" blends into the noise. Niche marketing wins.

2. **No proof**: Claims without results are empty. Collect testimonials, case studies, and before/after stories from the start. Even a single powerful testimonial is worth more than a hundred posts.

3. **Inconsistent**: Posting once and giving up, or posting daily for a week then disappearing, builds no audience. Sustainable consistency beats heroic bursts.

4. **Selling too soon**: Pitching a package in the first interaction before providing any value feels icky. Build trust first.

5. **No email list**: Social media platforms change algorithms and can delete your account. Your email list is the only audience you own. Start building it on day one.

## Building an Email List

Your email list is your most valuable marketing asset. Start building it before you have anything else.

- Offer a lead magnet: a free worksheet, a PDF guide, or a video training related to your niche.
- Use a simple landing page tool (or even a Google Form) to collect emails.
- Send a weekly or bi-weekly newsletter with one valuable insight, one resource, and one invitation.
- Never spam your list. Every email should feel like a gift.

Over time, your email list becomes a direct line to people who already know, like, and trust you. When you launch a new package or program, they are your first and most responsive audience.
